<ol><li>Rules<ol><li>The event is governed by the rules as defined in The Racing Rules of Sailing.</li></ol></li><li>Sailing Instructions<ol><li>The sailing instructions will be available no later than Saturday 5th March at [www.carrickfergussc.org](http://www.carrickfergussc.org).</li></ol></li><li>Communication<ol><li>The online official notice board is located at [www.carrickfergussc.org](http://www.carrickfergussc.org).</li><li>While racing, except in an emergency, a boat shall not make voice or data transmissions and shall not receive voice or data communication that is not available to all boats.</li></ol></li><li>Eligibility and Entry<ol><li>The event is open to all boats of the RS400 and Topper classes.</li><li>Boats may enter the event by registering online at [www.carrickfergussc.org](http://www.carrickfergussc.org).</li><li>To be considered an entry in the event, a boat shall complete all registration requirements and pay all fees.</li><li>Late entries will be accepted at the discretion of the Event Committee.</li></ol></li><li>Fees<ol><li>Entry fees are as follows:<br>Topper Member - £5<br>Topper Non-member - £10<br>RS400 Member - £15<br>RS400 Non-member - £25</li></ol></li><li>Schedule<ol><li>Dates of racing: Sunday 6th March to Sunday 10th April 2022.</li><li>Number of races: 18</li><li>The scheduled time of the warning signal for the first race each day is 14:00.</li><li>On the last scheduled day of racing no warning signal will be made after 16:00.</li></ol></li><li>Venue <ol><li>Racing will take place in Belfast Lough near Carrickfergus Sailing Club.</li></ol></li><li>Courses<ol><li>Windward/Leeward courses will be sailed.</li></ol></li><li>Penalty System<ol><li>RRS 44.1 is changed so that the Two-Turns Penalty is replaced by the One-Turn Penalty. </li><li>The Post-Race Penalty, Advisory Hearing and RYA Arbitration of the RYA Rules Disputes procedures shall apply (for information on the procedures, go to www.rya.org.uk/go/rules). The outcome of an RYA Arbitration can be referred to a protest committee, but an arbitration cannot be reopened or appealed.</li></ol></li><li>Scoring<ol><li>Three races are required to be completed to constitute a series.<br>(a) When fewer than 4 races have been completed, a boat’s series score will be the total of her race scores.<br>(b) When from 4 to 6 races have been completed, a boat’s series score will be the total of her race scores excluding her worst score.<br>(c) When from 7 to 9 races have been completed, a boat’s series score will be the total of her race scores excluding her two worst scores.<br>(d) When from 10 to 12 races have been completed, a boat’s series score will be the total of her race scores excluding her three worst scores.<br>(e) When from 13 to 15 races have been completed, a boat’s series score will be the total of her race scores excluding her four worst scores.<br>(f) When 16 or more races have been completed, a boat’s series score will be the total of her race scores excluding her five worst scores.</li><li>Rule A5.2 is changed so that, a boat that did not come to the starting area, came to the starting area but did not start, did not sail the course, did not finish, retired or was disqualified shall be scored points for the finishing place one more than the number of boats that came to the starting area.</li></ol></li><li>Risk Statement<ol><li>Rule 3 of the Racing Rules of Sailing states: ‘The responsibility for a boat's decision to participate in a race or to continue racing is hers alone.’</li><li>Sailing is by its nature an unpredictable sport and therefore inherently involves an element of risk.
